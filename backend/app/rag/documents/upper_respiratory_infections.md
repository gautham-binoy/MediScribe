# Clinical Guideline: Acute Upper Respiratory Tract Infection (URI) & Pharyngitis

## Overview
Upper respiratory tract infections (URIs) encompass acute viral rhinitis, rhinopharyngitis, and non-streptococcal pharyngitis. The vast majority (> 85%) of acute respiratory infections are viral in etiology (Rhinovirus, Adenovirus, Coronavirus, Parainfluenza).

## Clinical Features
- Symptoms: Mild fever, sore throat, odynophagia, nasal congestion, rhinorrhea, mild non-productive cough, malaise, tension-type frontal headache.
- Modified Centor Criteria for Group A Streptococcus (GAS):
  - Tonsillar exudates (+1)
  - Tender anterior cervical adenopathy (+1)
  - Absence of cough (+1)
  - History of fever > 38°C (+1)
  - Score 0-1: GAS infection very unlikely (< 10%); antibiotic therapy is NOT recommended.

## Investigations
- Routine lab tests are NOT routinely required for uncomplicated mild viral URI.
- If symptoms persist > 7-10 days or worsen with purulent sputum, order CBC and Chest X-ray to rule out secondary bacterial bronchitis or pneumonitis.

## Management Protocol
- **Primary Line**: Supportive and symptomatic care.
- **Antipyretic / Analgesic**: Paracetamol 650mg TDS PRN for fever, body ache, or sore throat.
- **Antihistamines**: Cetirizine 10mg once daily (OD) at bedtime for allergic rhinitis/rhinorrhea symptoms, or Levocetirizine 5mg OD.
- **Steam Inhalation & Saline Gargles**: Warm water gargling 3-4 times daily for pharyngeal irritation.
- **Antibiotic Stewardship**: Do NOT initiate empirical systemic antibiotics for clear viral URIs.
